Step by step

{% if censusType == "autumn" %}

  1. Step 1 : Check the census dates

    There are two important dates to know.

    ‘Census date’ is the date that your census return describes. Your return should reflect your school as it was on this day.

    ‘Return date’ is the deadline for submitting your return. If your school is part of a trust or maintained by a local authority (LA), they may set an earlier deadline. They will confirm this with you.

  2. Step 2 : Prepare the data in your MIS

    Data for the census is prepared and checked in your school’s MIS, not in COLLECT. COLLECT is only used to submit the return and fix any errors.

    There is guidance on data items covering everything the census collects this term. Your MIS supplier’s guidance will tell you where to record this data.

    Start preparing the data well before census day. You may need information from several people in your school, including your:

    • headteacher
    • special educational needs coordinator
    • staff responsible for catering and free school meals

  3. Step 3 : Available {{ "2026-09-03" | govukDate }} Test your data before you submit it

    COLLECT runs a “familiarisation period”, usually beginning 4 weeks before the census opens. During this time you can upload your census return file in advance, see any errors or queries it raises. Your school’s census contact will be emailed when this is available.

    Export the census return file from your MIS, upload it to COLLECT, and see what issues it identifies. Fix any problems in your MIS and generate a new return. You can do this as many times as you need until your return is error-free.

    See step 5 for guidance on resolving errors and queries .

  4. Step 4 : Generate your return and get headteacher sign-off

    When you’re confident that your census data is free of errors, generate the final return from your MIS.

    Your headteacher must check and authorise the return before you submit it. Make sure they have seen it, because submitting the return confirms that they have authorised it.

  5. Step 5 : Available {{ "2026-10-01" | govukDate }} Submit your return

    Upload your return to COLLECT. You will be told whether there are any errors or queries.

    'Errors' must be fixed before you can submit. Correct them in your MIS, export a new return and upload it again.

    'Queries' flag data that looks unusual. You must either correct them in your MIS, or explain why the data is correct by adding an explanation note in COLLECT.

    Notes for queries must give a detailed reason. Notes like ‘confirmed’ or ‘data is correct’ won’t be accepted for most queries, and will delay DfE approving your return.

    See guidance on:

    • viewing your errors and queries (opens in new tab)
    • adding explanation notes for queries (opens in new tab)
    • COLLECT queries and the explanatory notes DfE accepts (Excel sheet, 49KB)

    Once you have addressed any errors and queries, you can submit your return.

  6. Step 6 : Wait for DfE to check and authorise your return

    Once you have submitted your return, DfE will check and authorise it. This is the final confirmation that your return is complete.

    You will be notified by email when your return is authorised. Until then, you do not need to do anything unless we contact you about a problem.

    If a local authority or multi-academy trust approves your return on your behalf, they will check and approve it before sending it to DfE.

{% endif %} {% elseif censusType == "workforce" %}

  1. Step 1 : Check the census dates

    There are two important dates to know.

    ‘Census day’ is the day that your census return describes. Your return should reflect your school’s staff as they were on this day.

    The deadline is the last day you can submit your return. If your school is part of a trust or maintained by a local authority (LA), they may set an earlier deadline. They will confirm this with you.

  2. Step 2 : Check who prepares and submits your return

    How your return is prepared depends on your school. Your school may do everything itself, or your trust or LA may do some or all of it for you.

    Check who:

    • provides your staff records
    • prepares and submits your return
    • approves your return after it’s submitted

    If your school is part of a trust or maintained by an LA, check with them to find out how this works.

  3. Step 3 : Make sure your staff records are complete

    Your return is created from your staff records. These may be held in your management information system (MIS), HR or payroll systems.

    Check that:

    • all staff have up-to-date personal details, such as National Insurance numbers and teacher reference numbers
    • all current staff have up-to-date contract, pay and working hours details
    • staff who have left since the start of the last school year have a contract end date in their record
    • staff absences from the last school year have been recorded

    To check which staff to include, see section 4 of the school workforce census guide (opens in new tab).

    You may need support from whoever manages HR or payroll for your school. If your LA or trust holds some of your staff records, they may check these for you.

  4. Step 4 : Record your school's vacancies and staffing on census day

    Some information in your return is only about the state of your school on census day. On that day, record:

    • any vacant teaching posts
    • how many supply teachers are in school who have been with you for less than 28 days
    • staff who have left since the start of the last school year have a contract end date in their record
    • how many support staff are in school who are not employed directly by your school or LA, such as agency teaching assistants or contract cleaners

    If your LA or trust prepares your return, they may contact you for this information.

  5. Step 5 : Create and check your return

    Your return is usually created in your school’s MIS.

    Before you create your return, run any data checks your MIS offers. These can help you find and fix errors and inconsistencies early.

    Your MIS may also include a summary of your return. Ask someone who knows your staff records to check the summary, as comparing it to last year’s can help you spot anything unexpected. Your headteacher can then check the summary before your return is sent to your LA or DfE.

    If your LA or trust prepares your return, they may ask you to check it.

  6. Step 6 : Available {{ "2026-11-05" | govukDate }} Submit your return

    Upload your return to COLLECT. You will be told whether there are any errors or queries.

    ‘Errors’ must be fixed before you can submit. Correct them in your MIS, create a new return and upload it again.

    ‘Queries’ flag data that looks unusual. You must either correct the data, or explain why it’s correct or missing by adding a note in COLLECT. If any queries are not explained, DfE cannot authorise your return.

    For many queries, DfE provides the exact note to use, starting with the query number. Check the notes DfE accepts for each query in the school workforce minimum notepad entries guidance (opens in new tab) before writing your own.

    If your return comes from more than one system, such as your MIS and a separate HR system, the files need to be combined in COLLECT before you can submit. This is called ‘matching and reconciliation’.

    Once you have addressed any errors and queries, and combined your files if you need to, you can submit your return.

    If your school is maintained by an LA, they will tell you how to submit. You may need to send your return to them instead. They will also usually do any matching and reconciliation for you.

  7. Step 7 : Wait for your return to be checked and authorised

    After you submit your return, it needs to be ‘approved’ and then ‘authorised’.

    ‘Approved’ means whoever is responsible for your return has accepted it. If your school is maintained by an LA, your LA checks your return and approves it. They may also add data from their own systems. If your school is an academy, you are responsible for approving your own return.

    ‘Authorised’ means DfE has checked and accepted your return. If they have questions or need changes, they will contact your LA or academy. This is usually the final stage.

    You will get an email when your return has been authorised. Until then, you do not need to do anything unless DfE or your LA contacts you about a problem.

    DfE may run further checks after authorising your return. If they find a problem, they will contact your LA or academy.
